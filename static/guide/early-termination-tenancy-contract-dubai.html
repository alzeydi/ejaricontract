Breaking a lease in Dubai:
early termination rules, penalty and steps

Updated October 2026 · Law 26/2007 as amended by Law 33/2008 · checked against DLD and RDC pages

Want to leave your Dubai rental before the contract ends? Dubai law gives tenants no automatic right to break a lease and sets no penalty — the cost comes from your own contract, usually 1–2 months’ rent plus 60–90 days’ notice, and without such a clause you need the landlord’s agreement. This guide shows how to check your clause, negotiate the exit, protect your cheques and deposit, close Ejari and DEWA, and what the landlord can and can’t do in return.

i

The short answer: your contract decides, not a law

Article 7 of Law No. 26 of 2007 says a valid lease “may not be unilaterally terminated during its term by the Landlord or the Tenant” — only by mutual consent or on the grounds the law lists. There is no statutory right to leave early and no statutory penalty. What you pay depends on three things:
Your contract has an early-termination clauseThat clause applies — usually 60–90 days’ notice plus 1–2 months’ rent. This is market convention, not law.
No clause at allYou need the landlord’s agreement. Everything — date, amount, deposit, cheques — is negotiated.
Exceptional hardship (job loss, illness)No automatic right. The Rental Dispute Centre can relieve you case by case, often against a payment.
The DLD Unified Tenancy Contract itself contains no early-termination penalty. If you have one, it was added in the “Additional Terms” box or in an addendum — check there first.

Myth: “90 days’ notice is enough to leave”

It isn’t. Article 14 (as amended by Law 33 of 2008) requires 90 days’ notice before expiry to change the terms at renewal — for example a rent increase. It does not give a tenant the right to end a running contract early, and several popular property sites still say otherwise. Notice periods for leaving early come only from your contract.
1

Read the Additional Terms and any addendum

Look for words like “early termination”, “pre-termination”, “vacating before expiry” or “penalty”. Note three numbers: the notice period, the penalty (months of rent or a fixed amount) and anything said about the deposit. Clause 12 of the unified contract adds that “any additional condition will not be considered in case it conflicts with law” — a penalty clause itself is lawful, but it cannot, for example, strip your deposit for normal wear and tear.
2

Give written notice and make an offer

Email and WhatsApp are fine for negotiating, but send your notice in writing and keep proof. Make the landlord’s decision easy:
  • Offer a replacement tenant — the most common way to get the penalty reduced or waived.
  • Propose a handover date and a specific amount rather than asking “what do I owe?”.
  • Keep paying rent until the agreed date — stopping payments gives the landlord grounds to sue for the full term.
3

Sign a termination agreement — list every cheque

Put the deal on one page signed by both sides: end date, penalty amount and how it is paid, what happens to the security deposit, who cancels Ejari and when, the DEWA final bill, and a mutual release of further claims. List each post-dated cheque to be returned by number and date. There is no statutory rule obliging the landlord to hand cheques back without such an agreement.
Don’t order your bank to stop a cheque. Bounced cheques for insufficient funds were decriminalised in 2022, but a stop-payment instruction without legal reason is still treated as bad faith — and a returned cheque can be enforced directly through the courts.

4

Close utilities and Ejari

  • DEWA: submit Move-to if you are moving within Dubai (your deposit transfers), or Move Out if you are leaving — AED 125 for a small meter plus AED 20 in fees; the DEWA deposit is offset against the final bill and the balance refunded to your IBAN.
  • Ejari: cancellation is free on Dubai REST (AED 40 + VAT at a trustee centre). For a contract that hasn’t expired yet, DLD asks for a letter from the owner requesting cancellation — another reason to get the termination agreement signed first. See how to cancel Ejari.
i

Your security deposit when you leave early

Article 20 says the landlord holds the deposit “to ensure maintenance of the Real Property” and refunds it, or what remains, at the end of the lease. Legally it covers damage beyond ordinary wear and tear, not your penalty. In practice many landlords offset it against the penalty — fine if you both agree, but write it into the termination agreement. If the landlord simply keeps it, see getting your deposit back.
i

Can the landlord end your lease early?

Only on the grounds in Article 25(1) as amended by Law 33 of 2008, with notice served through a notary public or by registered mail:
  • rent unpaid 30 days after a formal notice;
  • subletting without written consent; illegal or immoral use; use for a different purpose;
  • dangerous alterations or deliberate damage; a Dubai Municipality report that the building may collapse; a government demolition order;
  • any other legal or contractual breach not fixed within 30 days of notice.
Selling, moving in a relative or renovating are grounds only at expiry, with 12 months’ notarised notice — see eviction notice rules. A sale on its own never ends your lease (Article 28), and the landlord may not cut services to push you out (Article 34).
i

Job loss, leaving the UAE, death

  • Death of the tenant — the only express statutory exit. Under Article 27 the lease continues with the heirs, who may end it with 30 days’ notice to the landlord.
  • Job loss or relocation — no automatic right. The RDC has let tenants out in hardship cases (for example during COVID-19, and in one reported case against one month’s rent), but each case turns on its facts. Bring your termination letter or visa cancellation.
  • Subletting instead of leaving — allowed only with the landlord’s written consent (Article 24).
⚖

No deal? The RDC route

File at the Rental Dispute Centre. The fee for termination claims is 3.5% of annual rent, minimum AED 500, maximum AED 20,000 (plus small service fees). Cases start with amicable settlement; if you settle there, half of the basic fee is refunded. Documents must be in Arabic or legally translated, and your contract must be registered on Ejari.

Frequently asked questions

Can I break my tenancy contract early in Dubai?

Only with the landlord’s agreement or under a termination clause in your contract. Article 7 of Law 26/2007 says a valid lease cannot be ended unilaterally during its term, and there is no general statutory right for tenants to leave early.

What is the penalty for breaking a lease in Dubai?

The law sets none. The penalty comes from your contract’s Additional Terms or addendum; the market norm is one to two months’ rent plus 60–90 days’ notice. If your contract has no clause, the amount is whatever you agree with the landlord.

Is 90 days’ notice enough to leave my apartment in Dubai?

Not by law. The 90-day rule in Article 14 is about changing terms at renewal. A notice period for leaving early exists only if your contract contains one.

Will I get my post-dated cheques back if I leave early?

Only if the landlord agrees. List each cheque by number in a signed termination agreement. Do not instruct your bank to stop a cheque — that is still treated as bad faith.

Can my landlord keep the security deposit if I terminate early?

The deposit legally secures repairs beyond normal wear and tear. Landlords often offset it against the penalty; that is fine if you both agree it in writing. Otherwise you can claim it back at the RDC.

Can I end my lease early if I lose my job or leave the UAE?

There is no automatic right. The RDC can grant relief in genuine hardship cases, sometimes against a payment, so try to negotiate first and keep your termination or visa-cancellation documents.

Can my landlord terminate my contract early?

Only on Article 25(1) grounds — such as rent unpaid 30 days after formal notice or unauthorised subletting — with notice by notary public or registered mail. Sale, own use or renovation need 12 months’ notice and apply only at expiry.
