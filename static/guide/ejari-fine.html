Ejari fines: what late registration
or renewal actually costs you

Updated August 2026 · Renewal fee stays AED 122.50 · No published late fine — but real blocks

Is there a fine for late Ejari registration or renewal? For tenants, no fixed fine is published and the fee stays AED 122.50 however late you are. The real penalty is what an expired or missing Ejari blocks: GDRFA visa applications, DEWA connections and changes, school registration, and Rental Dispute Centre filings. For landlords and agents, operating unregistered tenancies violates Law No. 26 of 2007, with penalties that can reach AED 50,000. Here is exactly what breaks, who bears the risk, and how to fix an expired Ejari today.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a fine for not registering Ejari in Dubai?

There is no automatic per-day fine charged to tenants, but operating without Ejari violates Law No. 26 of 2007 and regulated parties (landlords, agents, property managers) face penalties that can reach AED 50,000. For tenants the real, immediate cost is blocked services: no DEWA connection, no visa processing, no RDC case filing.

Is there a fine for late Ejari renewal?

No fixed late-renewal fine is published, and the renewal fee stays AED 122.50 no matter how late you are. The penalty is indirect: from the day your Ejari expires, GDRFA visa applications, DEWA changes, school registrations and Rental Dispute Centre filings are blocked until you renew.

What happens if my Ejari expired months ago?

Nothing retroactive — sign the renewed contract, renew Ejari as normal for AED 122.50, and services unblock immediately. There is no separate late-renewal procedure and no back-fees. The sooner you renew, the sooner visas, DEWA and RDC access work again.

Who is responsible for registering Ejari — and who gets fined?

Law No. 26 of 2007 places the registration duty on the landlord, but contracts routinely delegate it to the tenant, and either party can register. Regulatory fines target landlords, agents and management companies that operate unregistered tenancies; tenants suffer through blocked services rather than direct fines.

Can I file a rental dispute if my Ejari was never registered?

Registering late and then filing is the clean path — the RDC expects a registered tenancy. An unregistered contract weakens your standing and the case may be rejected until you produce an Ejari certificate, so register (even mid-dispute) before filing.
