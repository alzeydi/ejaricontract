Tenant rights in Dubai:
12 rules from the rental law

Updated November 2026 · Law 26/2007 as amended by Law 33/2008, Decree 43/2013 · checked against dlp.dubai.gov.ae and rdc.gov.ae on 16 Nov 2026

What rights does a tenant have in Dubai? Law No. 26 of 2007 gives you a written, Ejari-registered lease that neither side can end alone, repairs by the landlord unless agreed otherwise, eviction only on listed grounds with proper notice, a lease that survives a sale, and your deposit back. Below are the 12 rules, the article behind each one, and what to do when a landlord breaks them.

Each right, with the article and what it means

  1. A written lease with the essentials (Art. 4 (as amended 2008)) — Property description, purpose, term, rent and payment method, and the owner’s name if the landlord is not the owner. Must be registered with RERA (Ejari). Tenancy contract →
  2. A property in good condition at handover (Art. 15) — The landlord must hand over the property in a condition that lets you use it fully as agreed. Maintenance rules →
  3. Repairs by the landlord (Arts. 16–17) — Unless the contract says otherwise, the landlord maintains the property and repairs defects that affect your use, and bears wear and tear you did not cause. Who pays for repairs →
  4. No unilateral termination (Art. 7) — A valid lease cannot be ended early by one side alone — only by mutual consent or under the law. Breaking a lease →
  5. 90 days’ notice before terms or rent change (Arts. 13–14 (as amended)) — Either side wanting to amend terms at renewal must notify the other at least 90 days before expiry, unless agreed otherwise. Rent increase rules →
  6. Rent increases capped by the RERA index (Decree 43/2013) — At renewal the allowed increase depends on how far your rent is below the RERA average for similar units. RERA calculator →
  7. Automatic renewal if nobody objects (Art. 6) — Stay on after expiry without objection and the lease renews for the same term or one year, whichever is shorter, on the same terms. Ejari renewal →
  8. Eviction only on listed grounds (Art. 25 (as amended)) — During the term: e.g. unpaid rent 30 days after a notice to pay, unauthorised subletting. At expiry: demolition, major repairs, owner’s own use, or sale — with 12 months’ notice by notary or registered mail. Eviction notice →
  9. Compensation if “own use” was a pretext (Art. 26 (as amended)) — After repossession for own use, the landlord may not re-let a residential unit to a third party for 2 years (3 for non-residential), or the tenant can claim fair compensation. 12-month rule →
  10. The lease survives a sale (Art. 28) — A new owner takes the property subject to your fixed-term lease. Landlord selling →
  11. Your deposit back, minus real damage (Arts. 20–21) — The deposit secures maintenance at the end of the lease and must be refunded or its remainder returned; ordinary wear and tear is not your cost. Deposit refund →
  12. No cut-off of services, no harassment (Art. 34) — The landlord may not disconnect services or disturb your use. You can go to the police station with jurisdiction and claim damages at the tribunal. File at the RDC →

How to enforce your rights

  1. Write first. Send the landlord or agent a dated message quoting the article and asking for a fix by a date. Keep the thread.
  2. Check the paperwork. Your Ejari must be valid — check Ejari online — and the landlord must be the owner or authorised by the owner: verify the title deed.
  3. Services cut? Article 34 lets you go to the police station with jurisdiction and file a report.
  4. File at the Rental Disputes Centre. Fee: 3.5% of annual rent (AED 500 minimum, AED 20,000 maximum) for eviction, renewal, rent and termination cases; 3.5% of the amount claimed (AED 500–15,000) for money claims; plus AED 100 process service and AED 10 knowledge fee (RDC fee page, checked 16 Nov 2026). Common myths that cost tenants money

  • “I can leave with 90 days’ notice and no penalty.” Not by law: Article 7 bars unilateral termination; any early-exit right comes from your contract.
  • “I can stop paying rent until the AC is fixed.” Risky: withholding rent can trigger a 30-day notice to pay under Article 25(1)(a).
  • “A new owner can evict me straight away.” No: a fixed-term lease survives the sale (Article 28); eviction for sale needs 12 months’ notice at expiry.
  • “Rent can go up any amount at renewal.” Increases follow the RERA index under Decree 43/2013 and need 90 days’ notice.

Frequently asked questions

What are my rights as a tenant in Dubai?

A written lease registered in Ejari (Art. 4), a property handed over in good condition (Art. 15), repairs by the landlord unless agreed otherwise (Art. 16), no unilateral termination (Art. 7), eviction only on the grounds in Art. 25 with proper notice, a lease that survives a sale (Art. 28), your deposit back (Art. 20) and no service cut-offs (Art. 34).

Can my landlord evict me before the contract ends?

Only on the grounds listed in Article 25(1), such as unpaid rent 30 days after a notice to pay, subletting without written consent, illegal use or serious damage. The notice must go through a notary public or registered mail.

How much notice must a landlord give to evict me at the end of the lease?

12 months, served by notary public or registered mail, and only for one of four reasons: demolition, major repairs, the owner’s or a first-degree relative’s own use, or sale (Article 25(2) as amended by Law 33/2008).

Can my landlord increase the rent whenever they want?

No. Changes at renewal need at least 90 days’ notice before expiry (Article 14), and the increase is capped by the RERA rental index under Decree 43/2013. Check your figure with the rent increase calculator.

Can my landlord enter or cut my electricity?

Article 34 forbids the landlord from disconnecting services or disturbing your use of the property. You can go to the police station with jurisdiction and claim damages before the tribunal.

Where do I complain about my landlord in Dubai?

At the Rental Disputes Centre (rdc.gov.ae). The fee is 3.5% of annual rent (AED 500–20,000) for lease cases or 3.5% of the claimed amount (AED 500–15,000) for money claims, plus AED 100 process service.

Does the tenant have to pay for maintenance?

Day-to-day care, yes (Article 19). Maintenance and repairs of defects are the landlord’s by default (Article 16), but the contract can shift some of them to you.
