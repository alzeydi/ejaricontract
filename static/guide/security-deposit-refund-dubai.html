Landlord not returning your deposit?
How to get it back in Dubai

Updated August 2026 · Typical deposit: 5–10% of annual rent · RDC fee: min AED 500

How do you get your security deposit back in Dubai? Document the handover with photos and a report, send the landlord a written demand with a 7–14 day deadline, and if they refuse or go silent, file a case at the Rental Dispute Centre (rdc.gov.ae) for a minimum fee of AED 500. Landlords may only deduct for actual damage beyond normal wear and tear — and tenants with a clean handover file win these cases routinely. This guide covers what is deductible, the exact escalation steps, and how the RDC case runs.

i

What the landlord can — and cannot — deduct

The deposit guarantees the property is returned in the condition it was received, allowing for normal use. That single sentence decides most disputes:
Legitimate deductionsBroken fixtures, holes in walls, stained or burnt flooring, missing items listed in the inventory, unpaid rent or final utility bills
NOT deductible (normal wear)Repainting after a standard tenancy, minor scuffs and nail holes, worn carpet, sun-faded curtains, ordinary limescale
Typical deposit5% of annual rent (unfurnished) · 10% (furnished)
The landlord must show an itemised breakdown with receipts for any deduction. A blanket "cleaning and painting" deduction with no invoices does not survive an RDC hearing.
1

Protect yourself at move-out (before the dispute)

  • Do a joint inspection with the landlord or agent and get the handover report signed.
  • Take date-stamped photos/videos of every room, appliance and meter reading.
  • Return keys and access cards against a written receipt.
  • Settle the final DEWA bill and keep the closing statement — see DEWA move-out.
  • Cancel the old Ejari so the landlord cannot claim an open tenancy — it's free and takes minutes.
2

Send a written demand with a deadline

Email (or WhatsApp, with screenshots kept) a short, factual demand: the amount, the handover date, a note that the property was returned in normal condition, and a 7–14 day deadline. Attach the handover report and photos. State plainly that you will file at the Rental Dispute Centre if the deadline passes.
This message does double duty: it often gets you paid, and if not, it becomes Exhibit A that you tried to resolve the matter amicably — something RDC arbitrators explicitly look for.

File at the Rental Dispute Centre

File online at rdc.gov.ae with: the tenancy contract, Ejari certificate, handover report, photos, payment proof and your written demand. The fee is 3.5% of the claim, minimum AED 500 — for a typical deposit the minimum applies. First hearing lands within 7–15 days; most deposit cases are decided in a single session, and the tribunal can order the landlord to reimburse your filing fee.

Frequently asked questions

What can I do if my landlord is not returning my deposit in Dubai?

Send a written demand with a deadline (7–14 days is reasonable), attach the handover report and photos, and keep proof of delivery. If the landlord still refuses or goes silent, file a case at the Rental Dispute Centre via rdc.gov.ae — deposit cases are among the most common and fastest RDC filings, and tenants win when the property was returned in normal condition.

How long does a landlord have to return the deposit in Dubai?

The law does not set a fixed number of days, so the contract governs. Courts treat a 'reasonable period' as roughly 14–30 days after handover. Anything beyond that without an itemised justification supports an RDC claim.

What can a landlord legally deduct from a security deposit?

Only the cost of repairing actual damage beyond normal wear and tear, and any unpaid amounts under the contract (rent, utility bills the tenant owed). Repainting after a normal tenancy, minor scuffs and worn carpets count as ordinary wear — they are the landlord's cost, not yours.

How much does it cost to file a deposit case at the RDC?

3.5% of the claimed amount with a minimum of AED 500. For a typical AED 5,000–10,000 deposit the minimum AED 500 fee applies. If you win, the tribunal can order the landlord to reimburse the fee.

Do I need my Ejari certificate to claim the deposit back?

An active or recently expired Ejari registration strengthens the case substantially, and the RDC expects one. If the tenancy was never registered you can still file, but you must prove the tenancy with the signed contract and payment records.

How much is a security deposit in Dubai usually?

By market convention, 5% of annual rent for unfurnished and 10% for furnished properties. The amount and refund terms should be written into the tenancy contract — that figure is what you claim back.
